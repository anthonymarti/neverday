# Neverday business brief

## Decision and first customer

A branded retailer of finite data-only travel eSIM packs for U.S.-based independent travelers with an unlocked eSIM-compatible phone and a 7–15 day Japan trip. Tokyo/Kyoto/Osaka itineraries are the first acquisition focus, not guaranteed network coverage. Japan is a focused founder hypothesis: a memorable destination, clear arrival-navigation need and a useful 5 GB/15-day product to test. Demand and competitiveness still require interviews.

Neverday does not own radio infrastructure. Proposed wholesale supply is eSIM Access, conditional on actual approval and terms. Wider demo destinations are illustrative.

Positioning: **Go a little further.** Compatibility before payment; one finite price/quota; installation guidance before departure; visible balance/expiry; manual top-ups; no automatic renewal. Existing home service remains separate and may charge for calls/SMS/roaming. Neverday offers no cellular voice, SMS, number, porting or emergency-calling service.

## Why this model

| Direction | Budget and product assessment |
|---|---|
| Travel-data reseller | Per-pack ordering and a narrow first destination keep cost/support exposure understandable. Prepaid supplier funding still requires cash. Selected conditional commercial model. |
| Domestic service via MVNE / light MVNO | Voice/SMS/numbers/porting/emergency calling, taxes, support and carrier/MVNE agreements add obligations. Public $0 new-business terms were not verified. Defer. |
| Full MVNO | More core-network/control responsibilities, vendor contracts and operations. Incompatible with this budget. |
| Recurring global data | Destination mix, roaming rules, heavy usage and fair-use costs need contracted rates and experience. No blanket unlimited promise. Defer. |
| Affiliate/referral | Potential nearest $0-inventory route if approved: supplier is seller and collects payment. Less control/margin and a different customer checkout. Practical validation bridge, not branded service fulfillment. |

Mint is a domestic cellular benchmark. Popcorn advertises a $69 U.S. phone plan with global roaming, number transfer, talk/text and backup mechanisms. Neither is proof that a new branded reseller can obtain their economics for $0. Current eSIM Go minimum is $10,000/month; it is excluded. eSIM Access no-MOQ advertising fits smaller scale, but its API documents deposits/prepayment and no sandbox. Its public terms say wallet deposits cannot be refunded and agreement begins on account creation, so no account was created. See PROVIDERS.md.

## Offer and pricing hypothesis

Japan 3 GB/15days $7;5 GB/30 days $11;10 GB/30 days $18. Manual 3 GB top-up $7. Retail prices/behavior are independently chosen hypotheses, not live offers or validated competitive prices. Durations now match the public Japan list benchmarks in PROVIDERS.md; rates are published list figures, not approved Neverday terms.

Revenue would be retail margin on contracted packs and eligible top-ups. Avoid unlimited claims, auto-renewal and voice bundles. Initial refund policy in the demo permits unused/preactivation refunds; the real policy needs supplier eligibility, consumer law and fee funding. Do not launch an unbacked refund promise.

## No verified $0 commercial launch

**An immediate branded launch funded solely from each customer's card receipt is not verified and is not feasible under the documented prepaid candidate workflow without bridge funds.** No supplier postpaid credit was approved. Stripe typically schedules first payouts 7–14 days; risk holds may be longer. Capture success is not settlement availability.

If the published $2.70 benchmark applies to the approved first5GB order, it still needs supplier funds in advance, plus the ACTUAL wallet funding floor/deposit, funding fees, taxes, service-test cost, and refund/dispute reserves. The exact unavoidable dollar requirement is unknown because no approved account-specific order/funding terms or wallet minimum is verified. Do not present $2.70 as a real launch budget.

Ten5GB orders/day over an assumed14-day first-payout gap at the $2.70 public benchmark creates $378 supplier bridge need before deposits, tax, FX, processor fees and reserves. Normal settlement lag, weekends, bank delay and holds create ongoing needs after that. A practical spreadsheet should model daily available balance and maximum cash deficit, not only gross margin.

Possible routes:
1. Approved affiliate pilot: provider remains seller/collector and funds its own delivery. Airalo publicly advertises10% of final sale after discounts, paid following month's28th with $15 threshold (PayPal extra2%). At an illustrative $14 net sale, commission is $1.40 and11 such sales reach$15.40; this is not validated acquisition margin or a quoted Airalo pack price. Verify zero setup/minimum, program acceptance, disclosures and business/tax costs first.
2. Written supplier credit with no deposits/minimums and due dates safely after actual settlement; not obtained.
3. A funded small reseller pilot with explicit wallet and risk reserve; cannot happen under the current no-spending instruction.

An existing business credit line/card could bridge prepayment if supplier card funding, statement/grace period, limits and processor settlements align. This is financing and risk-bearing, not a verified $0 service cost or approved credit arrangement; no card or finance product was activated.

Charging well before departure and provisioning after cleared funds may reduce the gap, but creates delivery-delay/refund obligations and still needs reserves. Do not rely on it without written supplier/processor and consumer-law validation.

## Acquisition and validation

Use existing relationships and opt-in Japan travel communities; no unsolicited outreach or ad spending was executed. Create an original arrival checklist and compatibility guide. Interview 15 travelers with booked trips, then observe five full demo journeys. Recruit the first 20 funded pilot trips with manual onboarding support once launch gates pass.

Hypothesized gates: at least five of 15 interviewees want help and accept the proposed price; at least four of five observed demo users finish unaided; fewer than 10% compatibility mistakes in pilot; actual contribution after support/acquisition stays positive. These are proposed decision thresholds, not results. Count founder hours and partner commission as acquisition cost.

## Before the first paying subscriber

1. Choose/verify legal entity, actual operating jurisdiction and permitted target countries. U.S. customer focus does not establish the owner's location.
2. Supplier KYC/onboarding, executed reseller rights and rate/coverage/refund agreement; confirm actual wallet floor/credit, API rights and testing costs.
3. Processor approval for this business, actual settlements/reserves and refund/dispute arrangements.
4. Legal/tax classification: determine applicable FCC/USAC/state telecom, privacy/consumer, sanctions and sales-tax obligations; destination roaming restrictions. [USAC](https://www.usac.org/service-providers/contributing-to-the-usf/who-must-contribute/) lists cellular/local resellers and explains that some contribution exemptions still require Form 499-A. Data-only is not a universal exemption.
5. Fund immediate supplier fulfillment and realistic refund/dispute exposure; test authorized real service on relevant unlocked model/region variants and destinations.
6. Secure server-side pricing, payment webhooks, idempotent provisioning, genuine credential delivery, reconciliation, support and privacy practices.
7. Commercially eligible hosting. Connected Vercel team is Hobby; [Hobby terms](https://vercel.com/docs/plans/hobby) restrict it to personal non-commercial use. No paid upgrade or free commercial exception was obtained. Use an existing eligible plan or obtain explicit eligibility before a business launch.

No commercial agreement, live payment, paid API test or new paid resource was activated.
